## Rule

A novel interaction is adopted only when it measurably outperforms the familiar one for the user's task.

"It is fresher" or "everyone else does the boring thing" is a reason to prototype, not to ship.

## Why

Designers see many interfaces and tire of the familiar; users see this product briefly and lean on what they already know.

## Examples

- Keeping a standard form for checkout after a conversational alternative tested slower.
- Trying a new navigation model in a sandbox and comparing task completion with the current one.

## Exceptions

Brand or editorial surfaces where delight is the task may take the novel option deliberately.
