## Rule

An option that adds cost, shares data, extends a commitment or reduces privacy starts off. Defaults may favour the user's likely intent, never the business's revenue at the user's expense.

## Why

Most people accept defaults; a preselected paid or invasive option converts inattention into consent.

## Examples

- Marketing consent unchecked; the expensive shipping option not preselected.
- The recommended plan highlighted but not pre-chosen.

## Exceptions

Safe, reversible defaults that protect the user (two-factor prompts, autosave) may start on.
